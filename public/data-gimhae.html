01Starting point

12 points from a performance worth 22.1

Gimhae FC have 2W 8D 16L from 26 matches: 14 points, 20 scored, 50 conceded, 17th in the table. Take the shots from the 24 matches with event data, replay each match 20,000 times, and the expected points come to 22.1. The chance of finishing at or below their actual total is 2.4%.

The shortfall of −10.1 is the largest in the league. This piece asks where those points went. The short answer is two words — at home, and 0-0. The rest of it is phase by phase: what works, and where it breaks.

What this piece covers

  • League and table figures use all 26 matches (2W 8D 16L · 14 points).
  • Event-based figures use 24 matches. R25 at home to Jeonnam (2-2) has no event data in the portal, and R28 at home to Chungnam Asan (1-1, 10 Oct) falls outside the league comparison pool (216 matches up to 20 Sep). Both are home games.
  • So "11 home matches" below means the 11 of their 13 home games (0W 4D 9L · 4 points) that can be analysed.
  • Every rank is out of the 17 clubs in the division.
02Where it went

2 points from 11 home matches

In the 11 home matches with event data Gimhae are 0W 2D 9L for 2 points. The same matches were worth 11.2, and the chance of taking 2 or fewer is 0.3%. Away from home: 10 points from 13 matches, expected 10.9 — almost exactly as deserved.

So the shortfall is a home-only phenomenon. That is not the same as saying they played well at home. Per home match they create 1.08 non-penalty xG and allow 1.63, which is a losing profile. It is just not a 2-point one. By points per match they are 0.25 at home and 0.77 away — one of the few sides better away.

03When it breaks

While the game is level they allow more than anyone

Split each match by score state and the picture sharpens. While the score is level Gimhae allow 1.51 xG per 90, the highest in the league (average 0.85; Ansan are second at 1.13). In the same minutes they create 0.57, second lowest.

So they score the next goal from level only 17.2% of the time, against 27.5% implied by shot quality — 10.3 percentage points below. From level they have scored 5 (xG 9.0) and conceded 24 (xGA 23.7). They concede first, and that one sentence is the shape of the season: they have opened the scoring in only 16% of matches.

04The consequence

206 minutes ahead, and no goals scored while ahead

A side that concedes first rarely leads. Gimhae have held a lead for 206 minutes, the fewest in the league and two thirds of Cheonan's 312. Of the 24 matches analysed they led in 5 (they also scored first in R25 and R28, so the season figure is 7).

In those 5 matches they scored not one goal while ahead (xG 1.3). They lost a lead 3 times, conceding 3 goals from 3.5 xGA, so this is not a story of bad luck while protecting a lead. They have barely had a lead to protect. When they do score first they average 1.75 points a match, against 0.29 when they concede first — the first goal matters unusually much to this team.

05Then what

Chasing, they concede +4.5 goals more than expected

They have spent 1101 minutes behind. The attacking side of it is reasonable — 0.86 xG per 90 (mid-table), pass share up to 56%, the defensive line pushed up to 40.3, and final-third passes at 84.4 per 90, nearly double their baseline.

The problem is at the other end. Chasing, they have conceded 20 goals from 15.5 xGA. The +4.5 gap is the largest in the league. Going after an equaliser has repeatedly cost them a second and a third. In other words, the football they play when behind is ordinary; the price they pay for it is not.

06Timing

15 non-penalty goals conceded after 75′ — most in the league

The goals arrive at the end. Across the 24 matches analysed, the 15 non-penalty goals conceded after 75 minutes are the most in the league and +4.6 above the 10.4 xGA of those situations. That is 38% of their 40 non-penalty concessions, all in the last fifteen minutes and stoppage time.

In fifteen-minute blocks it is starker: 6 scored and 16 conceded between 76′ and the end; 21 conceded in the first half against 28 in the second. Goal difference per match is −0.56 before the break and −0.64 after.

These numbers cannot separate fitness from substitutions from concentration. They do say where to look first — the next job is a scene-by-scene list of those 16 goals.

07Phase 1

Build-up — the ball comes out, but it does not arrive

Take the journey from back to front in two steps. Of the attacks that start in their own third, 61.1% reach the middle third (15th in the league) and 26.9% reach the final third (16th). League averages are 62.9% and 30.0%.

To the middle third they are close to average; the gap opens on the last step. This is less a side that cannot circulate the ball than a side whose last forward pass keeps getting cut off. Forward passes make up 25.0% of their passing and long balls 15.4%, both at the low end of the league — they are not launching it either.

What the data says — build-up and progression

  • Own third → middle third 61.1% (15th) / league average 62.9%
  • Own third → final third 26.9% (16th)
  • Attacks starting in the middle third that reach the final third 44.0% (15th)
  • What they allow: 63.9% to the middle third and 30.3% to the final third (7th and 7th highest by volume allowed), so stopping the opponent's progression is mid-table
  • Final-third entries 28.8 per 90 (14th most) against 32.1 allowed (4th most)

Strengths and weaknesses of this phase

  • Works — Getting the ball to the middle third without losing it is close to league average.
  • Works — They do not throw possession away with long balls (15.4%).
  • Does not — Final-third entry rate is 16th in the league — the last step fails most often.
  • Does not — They reach the final third 3.3 fewer times per 90 than their opponents do.
08Phase 2

Final third — few shots in, and the shots do not go in

Of the attacks that reach the final third, 23.6% produce a shot (12th in the league), and each possession there is worth 0.024 xG (12th). They get in rarely, and they are below average once in.

The finishing is worse. Open-play xG runs at 0.69 per 90 (15th) against 0.45 goals (17th), a gap of −0.25 — 17th in the league. Over the season their non-penalty shots were worth 23.3 xG and produced 15 goals, −8.3. Shot locations are not unusual: average distance 17.3m, 61.4% of shots inside the box. With 24 matches it is too early to split luck from finishing, but one thing is clear — this is not a side creating plenty and missing. The chances themselves are scarce.

What the data says — final third and finishing

  • Final-third possession → shot 23.6% (12th) · xG per possession 0.024 (12th)
  • Open-play goals minus xG per 90 −0.25 (17th in the league)
  • Non-penalty totals: 15 goals − 23.3 xG = −8.3
  • They have won 1 penalty all season (0.029 xG per 90, 17th) and given away 6
  • Dribble success 34.3% · aerial duels won 45.8% · ground duels won 44.8%

Strengths and weaknesses of this phase

  • Works — Shot locations are fine — 61.4% inside the box, 17.3m average distance.
  • Works — Scoring is not concentrated in one player: no one is in double figures and 8 players have scored.
  • Does not — Open-play goals per 90 of 0.45 is last in the league.
  • Does not — Non-penalty finishing of −8.3 is one of the largest single losses in this sample.
  • Does not — 1 penalty won against 6 conceded.
09Phase 3

Transition — plenty of regains, but low down, and punished the moment they lose it

Attacks that begin with a regain come at 52.1 per 90, 4th in the league. That is not few. The problem is where and how fast. 78.7% of those regains happen in their own third (3rd highest). Only 2.2% of them produce a shot inside ten seconds (16th), and the xG created within fifteen seconds is 0.154 per 90 (14th).

The other direction is the opposite. When Gimhae lose the ball, opponents get a shot away within ten seconds 5.1% of the time — the highest in the league — and create 0.451 xG per 90 within fifteen seconds, again the highest. In short, their transition exchange rate is the worst in the division: slow going forward, fastest and sharpest coming back.

What the data says — transition

  • Regain-started attacks 52.1 per 90 (4th) · share won in their own third 78.7% (3rd)
  • Median regain position 31.7 on a 0–100 scale (3rd lowest) · 6.8 passes from regain to first shot (3rd most)
  • Their 15-second transition xG per 90 0.154 (14th) against 0.451 allowed (1st)
  • Opponent shots within ten seconds of a turnover 5.1% (1st) — league average 3.3%

Strengths and weaknesses of this phase

  • Works — The volume of regains is top-four — this is not a side that refuses to press.
  • Works — They do win the ball back inside their own block, so recovery there functions.
  • Does not — Regains happen deep (median 31.7), so the counter has a long way to travel.
  • Does not — 6.8 passes from regain to shot gives the opponent time to reset.
  • Does not — What they allow immediately after losing the ball is the most damaging in the league.
10Phase 4

Set pieces — small on both sides, and on the losing end

Set pieces are worth 0.149 xG per 90 to them (8th), close to average, and have produced 0.112 goals per 90 (13th). Over the season: 3 set-piece goals from 3.98 xG (−1.0) and 5 conceded from 3.58 xGA (+1.4). By type, 0 from corners, 2 from free kicks, 1 from throw-ins.

Penalties are the clearest gap: 1 won against 6 conceded, with the penalties they give away worth 0.175 xG per 90 (2nd in the league). Set pieces and penalties together are worth several points over a season.

  • Defending set pieces 5 conceded − 3.58 xGA = +1.4 (positive is a loss; 4th largest)

  • Works — 0 red cards and 2.12 yellows a match — discipline is not the issue.
  • Does not — Attacking set pieces are −1.0 goals against expectation, with 0 from corners.
  • Does not — Set-piece concessions run +1.4 above expectation.
  • Does not — A 1:6 penalty ledger — they give away far more than they win.
11Phase by state

The same team behaves like three different ones

Read those phases again by score state and Gimhae look like three sides.

  • Ahead (206 min) — pass share 35%, defensive line 30.9, 57% of defensive actions in their own third. This is their deepest setting, and it produces 5.7 shots per 90 against 11.4 allowed.
  • Level (1103 min) — pass share 41%, line 33.4. Shots 5.9 against 12.6, xG 0.57 against 1.51. It is where they spend most of the game and where the exchange rate is worst.
  • Behind (1101 min) — pass share 56%, line 40.3, pressing intensity at its peak (PPDA proxy down to 3.0 from 7.1 when ahead). Shots 9.5 against 8.2 — the only state in which they out-shoot the opponent.

In other words, they are at their most aggressive when losing and at their most passive when level. The trouble is that level is where most of the season happens — 46% of all minutes.

12Defence

Once inside the box, it is already a chance

They have faced 322 shots worth 38.0 xG, including 106 from the centre of the box and 38 from the six-yard area. Once an opponent reaches the final third, 29.2% of those possessions end in a shot (2nd in the league) and each is worth 0.040 xG — the highest in the league. Arriving is the same as creating against this defence.

Adjust for where the shots on target went and the expected concession is 37.3 against 46 actual: a gap of +3.7, 5th in the league. The adjustment ignores pace, distance and deflection; it answers only "were the shots that came to this part of the goal saved at league-average rate". And that +3.7 is only part of the −10.1 overall shortfall.

What the data says — defence

  • Opponent final-third entries 32.1 per 90 (4th most) → shot 29.2% (2nd) → xG per possession 0.040 (1st)
  • Open-play xG allowed per 90 1.29 (1st) · goals allowed per 90 1.34 (1st)
  • Share of shots faced that were on target 41.0% (3rd highest, average 37.8%) · of those, 31.1% were goals (4th highest, average 27.1%)
  • 38 six-yard shots faced → 13 goals · 106 from the centre of the box → 18 goals
  • Placement-adjusted goals minus expected +3.7 (5th in the league)

Strengths and weaknesses of this phase

  • Works — The rate at which they stop opponents in the middle third is mid-table, so the defence is not failing everywhere.
  • Works — While ahead they concede −0.5 goals against expectation — fewer, not more.
  • Does not — Once an opponent is in, the shot and xG conversion they allow is the highest in the league.
  • Does not — 144 of the shots they face come from inside the box (six-yard area plus central).
  • Does not — Even on placement-adjusted terms the loss is +3.7 goals.

16Rejected

Five explanations the data does not support

✕

"They cannot keep the ball"

They move it from their own third to the middle third 61.1% of the time against a league average of 62.9%. The break happens on the next step.

✕

"They do not press"

Regain-started attacks come at 52.1 per 90, 4th in the league. The issue is not the count but the position (78.7% of regains in their own third).

✕

"They cannot hold a lead"

3 goals conceded while ahead against 3.5 xGA. There is almost no loss in that phase. They have simply been ahead for only 206 minutes.

✕

"It is the goalkeeper"

The placement-adjusted +3.7 is a real loss and 5th in the league, but it is a fraction of the −10.1 shortfall. The rest happens while the game is level.

✕

"They are poor away from home"

10 points away against 10.9 expected — exactly as deserved. The shortfall is at home.
