SEOUL E-LAND · 2026 · K LEAGUE 2 · 26 MATCHES

They Get In, but They Cannot Shoot

Teams that face Seoul E-Land reach the final third and still fail to finish the move with a shot — more often than against anyone else in K League 2. At the other end, Seoul E-Land move the ball forward better than any side in the league. I put all 212 league matches on one scale and followed where this team takes hold of games, where it lets go, and who shapes that flow.

Results that match the performance

Seoul E-Land have 14 wins, 6 draws and 6 defeats from 26 matches — 48 points, 3rd in the table, level with second-placed Suwon FC.

Rate every match on the expected goals (xG) of each shot and their non-penalty expected goal difference is +0.47 per match — 2nd in the league, behind only the leaders Suwon Samsung (+0.63).

Their goals went in at almost exactly the expected rate. 363 shots worth 41.5 xG produced 43 goals (+1.5). Re-take the same shots at their xG 100,000 times and that many come up 42% of the time. Split by score state, every cell sits within 1.5 goals of expectation.

AttackBuild-up · progression · entry

The best in the league at moving the ball forward

I split the attack into phases and applied the same yardstick to all 17 teams. Seoul E-Land rank 1st to 3rd at every stage of moving the ball forward.

One number looks odd. Only 22.1% of their passes go forward — 16th in the league. The team that passes forward least moves forward best. Pass completion 81.8% (2nd), dribble success 40.0% (1st): they circulate sideways and backwards without losing it, and go when the gap opens.

Wherever the line goes, the answer holds. Attacks that get deep into Seoul E-Land’s half end without a shot more often than anywhere else in the league.

Opponents rarely get there in the first place. Their build-up reaches midfield 59.4% of the time, the 2nd lowest in the league, and they reach the final third 28.6 times per 90, the 4th fewest. Stopped once before the threshold, and again beyond it.

StructureAttack and defence side by side

Both halves of the same pitch

Lay attack and defence over each other and you can see which way Seoul E-Land’s games tilt.

Where they win the ball back differs too. Among chains that began with a ball win, the share starting in their own half is 71.7% — the lowest in the league. They win it higher up than anyone. Yet their pressing intensity (PPDA 5.15) is an ordinary 10th: not harder, just further forward.

Regains in their own third come 21.2 times per 90, below the league average of 22.8; regains in the attacking third come 4.8 times, above the average of 4.2.

StateLeading · level · trailing

Even when ahead, strongest when behind

Split the same metrics by score state and this team has three different faces.

Level, they create 1.29 xG per 90 — 1st in the league. Trailing, they allow 0.47, the fewest, and create the 2nd most. But leading, created (1.25) and allowed (1.22) are almost equal; allowed ranks 11th. This team is at its most ordinary when it is in front. 12 of their 30 goals conceded came while leading.

Twist444 minutes behind

Behind, they lock the opponent in

When Seoul E-Land are behind — that is, when the opponent is ahead — that opponent’s attack stalls more than against anyone else in the league.

In the 14 matches in which they trailed at some point they took 16 points — joint-most in the league, with Suwon FC. After conceding first they average 1.45 points per match, 3rd.

PeoplePass links · shot chains

Three players are the route

I counted who passes to whom, and who was involved in attacks that ended in a shot.

Changhwan Park, Inpyo Oh and Euller. The same names recur on all three measures — pass links, threshold crossings and shot chains. Starts: Changhwan Park 23, Inpyo Oh 23 (joint-most among outfield players), Euller 19.

SurpriseUp front

The team was better when its top scorer was off

I compared the team’s record with each player on and off the pitch (ON/OFF). Substitutes tend to come on when the team is behind, so I subtracted the team average for each score state and kept only what was left.

I then isolated the minutes when only one of the two was on.

Hyun Kim often comes off the bench, so half his minutes fall after the 60th — he may have met tired opponents. Comparing only minutes after the 60th: only Hyun Kim +1.61 (199 min), only Jaeyong Bak +0.13 (555 min). The gap remains, and it appears at both ends: with Hyun Kim on, the team creates more and allows less.

That does not make Jaeyong Bak “the problem”. He scored 10 from 8.8 xG, better than expected. Hyun Kim has started only 6 matches, and opponent strength is not controlled for. The data says one thing — the whole team looks different depending on which striker is on. How pressing height and defensive transitions change is a question for video.

SquadReliance on foreign players

Foreign players carry it; Korean players finish it

Using the player pages of the official K League website, I checked the nationality of all 563 players who have appeared in K League 2. 74 are foreign. Seoul E-Land have used 7: Euller (Brazil, 1864 min) · Osmar (Spain, 1369 min) · Carius (Brazil, 923 min) · Gabriel (Brazil, 555 min) · Geraldes (Portugal, 251 min) · Caio (Brazil, 185 min) · Iredale (Australia, 158 min).

The side that rotates most

For all 17 clubs I counted how many starters changed from the previous match.

The spine barely moves: Seongjun Min (26 starts), Changhwan Park and Inpyo Oh (23), Jiung Baek (22). Weighted by minutes, the squad’s average age is 26.9, the 5th youngest in the league.

DisciplineHypothesis log

What did not survive the test

Hypotheses from this analysis that the data did not support stay in, undeleted. All of them were mine.

✕

Seoul E-Land’s points come from finishing

Rejected. Goals +1.5 (P = 0.42), goals conceded as expected. Every score-state cell is within ±1.5 goals.

✕

The more of the ball, the more they win

Rejected. Match-level rank correlation between possession and points is −0.26 (P = 0.19) — if anything the other way, and mixed with score effects.

✕

Ahead, they lock the game down

Rejected. xG allowed while leading, 1.22 per 90, is 11th in the league. Instead of locking down they keep attacking (open-play goals per 90 while leading 1.33, 1st).

✕

A team that lives on penalties and set pieces

Rejected. 2 penalties (15th). Season set-piece goals, 5, match their xG (5.21). The one exception is set pieces while trailing.

✕

Foreign players carry the team

Rejected. The foreign share of chance creation (non-penalty xG), 21.1%, is 16th in the league; of shot-chain involvement, 15th. The one place they lead is passes across the threshold.

SummaryOn one page

They cannot shoot against them; nobody moves the ball forward better

The diagnosis narrows to one line — opponents get in but cannot shoot, and Seoul E-Land move the ball forward better than anyone. Their weak moment is when they are ahead.

One-page summary

Seoul E-Land 2026 · K League 2 · 26 matches · up to 19 September 2026
  1. 2nd on expected goal difference; results as expected. NPxGD +0.47. Goals +1.5 (P = 0.42).
  2. Opponents get in but cannot shoot. Entry → shot allowed 22.3%, the league’s lowest — at all three lines.
  3. Best at moving the ball forward. Build-up → midfield 67.2%, 1st; progression 2nd; entries 3rd. Share of forward passes: 16th.
  4. They win it furthest forward. Lowest share of regains in their own half.
  5. Even when ahead. xG allowed while leading 11th; 12 of 30 goals conceded while leading. Opponent 10-second counters jump to 4th only then.
  6. Behind, they lock the opponent in. Every progression metric of a leading opponent is the league’s lowest, plus set-piece goals per 90 of 0.81, 1st.
  7. The route: Changhwan Park, Inpyo Oh, Euller. Most frequent threshold link Inpyo Oh → Euller.
  8. The striker paradox. Only Jaeyong Bak +0.18 vs only Hyun Kim +1.12 (xG difference per 90) — non-overlapping 95% intervals, not causal.
  9. Among the lowest reliance on foreign players. Share of chance creation 16th; only passes across the threshold rank 3rd.
  10. Most rotation in the league. 3.12 starters changed per match; 25 different line-ups.
What this piece does not say

Whether this shape is the head coach’s design (event data records outcomes) · whether Hyun Kim or Jaeyong Bak is the better player (ON/OFF is a team record, not an individual one) · whether set pieces while trailing are skill (four goals, found by scanning many metrics) · opponent strength (not adjusted).
