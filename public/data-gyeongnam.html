How Not to Lose

Gyeongnam FC lost their goalkeeper six minutes into the season. In the 12 matches since he returned in R15, they have lost once — while the chances they conceded actually rose. I put all 212 league matches on one scale, then laid the match status of every game over its phases, to find out what “not losing” really means here.

Starting pointOpening day · keeper sent off

Six minutes

Gyeongnam FC’s 2026 season went wrong after six minutes. On 1 March, at home to Jeonnam in R1, Kihyun Lee — the 192 cm goalkeeper signed from Hwaseong over the winter — denied an obvious goal-scoring opportunity outside his box and was sent off. Ten men held out for most of the match and lost 1-4.

From the next match Bumsoo Lee kept goal. From R2 to R14 Gyeongnam went 4 wins, 3 draws, 6 defeats, 15 points — lower mid-table. Kihyun Lee did not start again until R15 against Yongin on 6 June.

The 12 matches since are where this piece begins: 4 wins, 7 draws, 1 defeat. The only loss was R21 against Daegu. In the September table Gyeongnam are 9th on 34 points.

One question, then: how did Gyeongnam stop losing? The data answers in layers — the flow inside matches (they fell behind less often), the last cell in front of goal, matches that drift towards a draw and the away 1-1s, and an attack that shoots from distance yet scores more than expected. And at the end it asks again what “not losing” actually means.

After R15 the chances Gyeongnam gave up rose rather than fell: shots against per match from 10.1 to 12.1, non-penalty xG against from 0.96 to 1.25. So where did the missing goals against go? Opponents did not shoot less. Their shots went in less.

The last cell

The portal records which cell of the goal each opponent shot on target went to — two rows (top, bottom) × three columns (left, centre, right). League-wide conversion by cell tells you how many goals balls into those cells produce on average (the placement expectation).

While Kihyun Lee was in goal he faced 45 non-penalty shots on target. Placement expectation: 13.1 goals. Actual: 8. He stopped around 5 more than an average keeper would have. With Bumsoo Lee in goal: 38 on target, placement expectation 11.7, 14 conceded (+2.3).

Rank the league’s 20 keepers (30+ shots on target) by goals minus placement expectation per 10 shots on target, and Kihyun Lee is 2nd. Bumsoo Lee is 16th.

It mattered most away. In the six away matches since R15: 27 shots on target, 4 conceded, placement expectation 7.6. The six home matches: 18 on target, 4 conceded, expectation 5.4.

Why this cannot be credited to one man · In the same period the share of opponents’ shots taken inside the box fell from 67.9% to 58.6% — the defence may simply have pushed shots further out. Placement expectation knows nothing of shot speed, distance, deflections or screening. Lee’s sample is 45 shots on target in 12 matches. The table says only that “balls into the same cells were stopped more often than the league average”.

By the flow of the score, they let wins slip more often than they rescued defeats. Of 10 draws, 6 came after scoring first and being pegged back, 3 after falling behind and levelling, and 1 finished 0-0. Narrow it to the 7 away 1-1s and it is 4 after scoring first, 3 after conceding first. 6 draws after scoring first is the 2nd-most in the league (Chungbuk Cheongju have 7).

In the draws where they scored first, Gyeongnam were outplayed while ahead — open-play xG 0.99 : 1.15 over 156 minutes. With a lead they dropped off, and conceded the equaliser from that deeper position. R12 at Seongnam was scored in the 97th minute and equalised in the 99th; at Chungbuk Cheongju in R23 they led for 23 minutes with 0 shot(s) of their own while the opponent built 0.53 xG, and the equaliser came.

The 3 draws after falling behind (R17 Chungnam Asan · R19 Jeonnam · R20 Gimpo) were all away matches since R15. Over 132 minutes behind, xG was 0.50 : 0.55 — they did not pile on pressure; they stopped conceding and took one chance.

By performance another layer appears. Draws in which they were outplayed by 0.3 non-penalty xG or more were R20 Gimpo (conceded first) · R22 Gimhae (scored first) · R23 Chungbuk Cheongju (scored first) — whatever the order of goals, these were matches they might have lost. There were also draws in which they had the edge (R5 Ansan · R17 Chungnam Asan).

It still stands that their 12 away matches went 1–8–3, with 7 1-1s — the most in the league (next best 3). All six away matches since R15 were drawn; their expected points add up to 5.9, and they took 6. Replay the same shots and those six matches produce 1.7 draws on average; all six being drawn comes up 0.02% of the time. Of those six, three were rescued after going behind, two were leads let go and one was 0-0 — they saved as many matches as they let slip, and the sum is exactly the expected points.

Across the season they have drawn 10 times, 3.3 more than expected — the 4th-largest excess in the league.

Shooting from distance ①xG per shot 0.082 · lowest in the league

An attack that cannot make chances

The other half of the draws lies in attack. Gyeongnam do not create many chances to win.

Their average shot is worth 0.082 xG, the lowest in the league. Average shot distance is 18.6 m, the 2nd-longest; 47.4% of their non-penalty shots come from outside the box (2nd), and only 4.4% from the 6-yard box (16th). xG per match is 1.02 (15th), and attacks reaching the box number 10.2 per match (16th).

Both routes to chances are blocked. xG from settled attacks is 0.21 per match — the lowest in the league — and xG within 15 seconds of a regain is also the lowest (0.16 per match). They do get forward quickly — 58.0% of final-third entries come within 10 seconds of the chain starting (2nd) — but the quick ball forward does not carry into the box, and the shot comes from where it stops.

Shooting from distance ②Goals − xG +5.4 · 2nd

And still they score

Yet they score more than expected: 5.4 goals above their shots’ xG, 2nd in the league. The size of their chances is the smallest; their finishing is near the top.

Where does the surplus come from? Outside the box, where they shoot most, 130 shots produced 5 goals from 4.7 xG — exactly as expected. Long shots did not fly in unusually often. The surplus is inside the box: 12 shots from the 6-yard box for 4 goals (xG 2.5), 56 from the wide parts of the box for 6 (xG 4.1 — the 3rd-largest excess in the league). They scored all 4 penalties. Goals from cross-assisted shots are 3.0 above xG (2nd).

Defence · transitionsFouls and yellow cards: 1st

They stop play

Gyeongnam break up play more than anyone. 13.6 fouls and 2.48 yellow cards per match — both the most in the league. They are also fouled the most, 13.7 per match. Their matches stop a lot.

Three red cards, all three straight reds for denying an obvious goal-scoring opportunity: R1 Kihyun Lee (6th minute at 0-0, lost 1-4), R16 Lucão (59th minute at 0-0, won 2-1 with ten men), R23 Jungwon Choi (61st minute at 1-1, finished 1-1). A defence that will stop the move with a foul at the last moment.

What they lack is the reflex to win the ball straight back. They regain it within five seconds of losing it 22.9% of the time (16th); after losing it in the opponent’s half, 20.4% — the lowest in the league. Rather than pressing to win it back, they drop off and, if needed, foul. So xG allowed within 15 seconds of losing the ball stays around average, 0.42 per match (9th).

Set pieces balance out: 5 scored, 5 conceded.

PeopleLowest foreign-player share in the league

Carried by home-grown players

Gyeongnam lean on foreign players less than any club in K League 2: they account for 10.9% of minutes, the lowest in the league, and the lowest shares of shots and expected goals too.

Instead they used a lot of players — 35 in 25 matches, the most in the league — and their minutes-weighted average age of 26.6 is the 2nd-youngest.

Hyunoh Kim is the focal point of the attack. Per 90, the team scored 1.11 goals in his 1628 minutes on the pitch and 0.60 in the 899 without him; it conceded 0.61 and 1.40. By non-penalty xG, though, the two are similar (0.75 : 0.95 with him, 0.82 : 0.96 without), so most of that gap comes from finishing and saves.

RejectedExplanations the data does not accept

Read it this way and you get it wrong

✕

“They defended better after R15”

Only half true. Goals conceded fell, but chances conceded (non-penalty xG) rose from 0.96 to 1.25 per match. What improved was the last cell in front of goal.

✕

“Kihyun Lee turned it round on his own”

In the same period the share of opponents’ shots from inside the box fell from 68% to 59%, and Gyeongnam began to keep the ball more. Goalkeeper and team changed together.

✕

“The 1-1s were matches they would have lost”

Less than half. Of 7 away 1-1s, 3 came after conceding first and 4 after scoring first. Across all draws, 6 came after scoring first — the larger group.

✕

“Lucky draws piled up”

In the six away matches since R15, points (6) almost exactly match expected points (5.9). Luck changed the shape of the points, not the amount — all of them draws.

✕

“They are weak away”

Since R15 they have not lost away (0W 6D 0L). What is weak is their ability to win away.

✕

“They are good finishers”

Long shots went in only as often as expected. The surplus is a few shots inside the box and the penalties. Their chance creation is near the bottom of the league.

✕

“They get sent off for rough play”

All three dismissals were straight reds for denying a goal-scoring opportunity — fouls at the last moment rather than rough challenges.

SummaryLast seven: four away · three home

From not losing to winning

Gyeongnam found a way not to lose. Fall behind less, and level when they do. Keep the ball more and leave the risk behind to the last cell in front of goal. The result: one defeat in 12 since R15, and matches that drift along at level. But it is not a way to win — of 10 draws, 6 came after they had scored first.

The last seven: R28 Cheonan (away) · R29 Chungnam Asan (home) · R30 Busan (away) · R31 Hwaseong (home) · R32 Suwon FC (away) · R33 Suwon Samsung (away) · R34 Seoul E-Land (home). R29 brings part 20’s Chungnam Asan to Gyeongnam — a team that cannot score when it keeps the ball away from home, against a team that keeps the ball and does not lose. Three things to watch:

  • Does the last cell keep holding? — 45 shots on target, 8 conceded: Kihyun Lee’s saving is among the best in the league. Numbers like that tend to drift back towards average over time, and when they do, the higher xG against (1.25 per match) turns into goals.
  • The half hour after taking the lead — 6 draws after scoring first, 2nd. Can they go for a second goal rather than dropping off? With the lowest xG per shot and box entries 16th, it will not be easy.
  • The cost of stopping play — most fouls and yellows, three red cards for denying goal-scoring chances. One more could decide a match.
