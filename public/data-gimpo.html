Home, at last

Gimpo FC began 2026 without a home. The pitch at the Solter Football Stadium was being completely re-turfed, so from R2 to R14 they played 13 consecutive away matches. The home opener came on 7 June, R15 against Jeonnam.

Three months on the road went well. Across those 13 matches: 6 wins, 5 draws, 2 defeats, 23 points — 1.77 per match. They came home in fifth, in the play-off places.

What came after is the problem. From R15, 12 matches: 2 wins, 6 draws, 4 defeats, 12 points — 1.00 per match. In the September table Gimpo are 7th.

The easy explanation would be “coming home broke their rhythm”. The data says something else. Gimpo play the best home football in the league. They just do not win.

Home performanceNon-penalty xG difference +0.61 · 1st

Strongest at home

Re-read the matches by shot quality and Gimpo’s 10 home games are the best home games in K League 2. Non-penalty expected goal difference per match (chances created minus chances conceded, by xG) is +0.61 — 1st of 17.

At home they create 1.53 xG a match and concede 0.93. Replaying the same shots at their xG gives 1.78 expected points per home match, also 1st.

Away they are ordinary: +0.05 per match over 15 away games, mid-table. Gimpo’s good football is concentrated at home.

Home resultsHome points per match 1.10 · 11th

And unable to win there

Yet the 10 home matches have brought 11 points, 1.10 per match, 11th in the league — 6.8 fewer than expected. Replay those 10 matches 20,000 times with the same shots and 11 points or fewer comes up 3.4% of the time.

Away it is the other way round: +1.7 against expectation. Over the season Gimpo are 5.1 points short of expected (−5.1, the 6th-largest shortfall in the league) — and all of it comes from home matches.

Dominate, and draw

Attack ①Regain chains · 15-second transition xG 1st

Win it, 15 seconds

Gimpo’s attacks start the moment they win the ball. Attacks that begin with a defensive regain come 54.7 times per 90, 1st in the league.

And they are quick. xG created within 15 seconds of winning the ball is 0.40 per 90, 1st; within 10 seconds it is 0.27, 2nd. 62.8% of Gimpo’s non-penalty xG comes from attacks that start with a regain — also 1st. In a test that shuffles 10-second transition xG across the league’s team-matches, a figure as high as Gimpo’s came up 2.6% of the time.

Where do they win it? Split the pitch into nine zones and Gimpo win the ball back in the middle third 23.3 times per 90 — the most in the league. They are above average in their own third (24.3, 4th) and in the final third (4.9, 4th) too. The attacks that follow are big ones — 3rd for xG from own-third regains, 3rd for xG from final-third regains.

⭐ At home, though, this weapon carries less of the load. The share of non-penalty xG from regain-started attacks is 79.0% away and 65.0% at home — a gap of 13.9 points, the largest of the 17 clubs. At home, chances built by moving the ball against a settled defence are worth 0.48 xG a match, more than double the away figure (0.19). Fast attacks have not disappeared (15-second transition xG per match: home 0.53 · away 0.40). A team that wins it and finishes it away also has to carry the slow attack at home.

Attack ②Central-box shot share 1st

As far as the middle

Gimpo’s shots gather in the middle. Of their non-penalty shots, 42.3% come from the central box (inside the width of the goal area, outside the 6-yard box) — 1st in the league (average 33.0%). But one step further in — the 6-yard box, right in front of goal — they take only 3.4% of their shots, the fewest in the league (average 8.2%).

The central box is where they shoot most and score least. Goals there are 3.7 below xG, 15th; conversion is 11.3%, 13th.

Split home and away and the story sharpens. They took 62 central-box shots in each. Away they scored as expected (+0.9); at home, xG 9.6 produced 5 goals. Of the home scoring shortfall (5.3 non-penalty goals), 4.6 comes from this one zone. Home 6-yard-box shots in 10 matches: 2 (away: 8).

Entries through the half-spaces end in a shot 31.9% of the time (5th). Entries down the wings: 17.3% (13th). Gimpo’s good chances come not from the wing but from the channel between the wing and the centre.

From wide, they cross a lot: 18.2 per 90, 3rd, with 21.2% completed (13th). But goals from cross-assisted shots are 2.1 below xG — 16th. Shots from set pieces are 2.5 below, 16th. Attacks that end in the air have not paid off (2 headed goals, both away).

Defence ①xG conceded: the 2nd-fewest in the league

Closing the middle

“Conceding more” does not mean a weak defence. Gimpo are one of the stingiest sides in K League 2.

Shots conceded per match 9.7, xG conceded 1.06 — both the 2nd-fewest in the league. Opponents move from midfield into the final third 42.2% of the time, the 2nd-lowest, and xG conceded inside the box is 0.79 a match, the 2nd-fewest.

The method is clear. Split the point where opponents enter Gimpo’s final third by lane: entries through the centre are 11.5%, the lowest in the league (average 14.5%), and entries through the half-spaces are 28.4%, the highest. Close the middle, push them outside. The few central entries that get through are small too: 0.024 xG per entry, the 3rd-smallest.

Defence ②Open-play goals conceded − xG: 2nd

But breached in front of goal

They concede little and still let in more. Open-play goals conceded exceed xG by +0.22 per 90 — the 2nd-largest excess in the league. Non-penalty goals conceded: 29, from opponent shots worth 24.2 xG.

The breach is right in front of goal. 10.0% of shots conceded come from the 6-yard box, 4th — few shots overall, but the closest ones get through. Goals conceded from the central box are 2.8 above xG, 3rd. Goals from cross-assisted shots are 3.4 above xG (3rd). Of the 29 non-penalty goals conceded, 8 came from the 6-yard box and 7 from shots worth less than 0.1 xG.

The paradox is sharpest when the score is level. Level, Gimpo hold opponents’ progression (midfield → final third 38.8%), shots after entry (21.6%) and 10-second counters after a turnover (xG 0.08 per 90) all to the lowest in the league. In the same state, open-play goals conceded exceed xG by +0.40 per 90 — the largest excess in the league. Goals conceded while level: 16, from shots worth 11.5 xG.

Defence ③On-target goals − placement expectation +7.1 · 2nd

The last cell

What happened when opponents’ shots were on target? The portal records which cell of the goal each on-target shot went to — two rows (top, bottom) × three columns (left, centre, right).

First I took, across all 212 league matches, the share of on-target shots in each cell that became goals. Top corners 43–47%, bottom corners 29–37%, centre 19% (top) and 13% (bottom). That gives a “placement expectation” — how many goals shots into those cells produce on average — to compare with the goals actually conceded.

Gimpo faced 74 non-penalty shots on target, the 3rd-fewest in the league. They conceded 29; the placement expectation is 21.9. The gap of +7.1 is the 2nd-largest in the league. Opponents did not pick the corners unusually well — the placement-expected save rate is 70.4%, about league average — but the actual save rate was 60.8%.

⭐ This is what separates home and away. At home: 29 shots on target, 14 goals, placement expectation 8.9 — a gap of +5.1. Away: 45 on target, 15 goals, expected 13.0 (+2.0). The home excess in goals conceded seen earlier (+4.7, penalties and own goals excluded) comes almost entirely from this one step.

More time behind than in front

For a side 5th on expected goal difference, Gimpo spend more time behind (25%) than in front (21%). They have drawn 11 times — 4.5 more than the 6.5 their shot quality suggests, the 3rd-largest excess in the league.

  • Leading — they shoot, but it does not go in. Entry → shot 32.4%, 3rd; 10- and 15-second transition xG both 2nd. Yet open-play goals are 0.46 below xG per 90 (15th). While leading: 4 goals from 6.3 xG. Points dropped from winning positions: 12.
  • Level — the tightest defence, and the most conceded. The paradox from the previous section lives here. In attack, regain chains are 58.2 per 90, 1st.
  • Trailing — progression works, dead balls do not. Build-ups reach midfield 61.3% of the time, 17th (lowest), but midfield → final third is 60.8%, 3rd. Set-piece xG is 0.06 per 90, 17th (lowest). Points per match after conceding first: 0.45, 14th.
PeopleForeign reliance · oldest squad · 5 red cards

Who moves this team

Gimpo’s shots come from their foreign forwards. 56.7% of the team’s shots and 61.5% of non-penalty goals belong to foreign players — both 1st in the league. Their share of minutes (28.0%) is 3rd, but their share of output is the biggest.

Luis Mina was the centre of it. 81 shots (27% of the team’s), 10 goals (3 penalties), xG 11.9. With him on the pitch the team created 1.16 xG per 90; without him, 0.72. His last appearance was R22 (16 August), after which he moved to Suwon Samsung. There have been only 4 matches without him, so this piece does not judge the effect of the transfer.

The squad is the oldest in the league: minutes-weighted average age 30.5, 1st. Substitutes have scored 3 goals, 10% of the team’s total, 16th. Red cards: 5, most in the league (average 1.3).

RejectedExplanations the data does not accept

Read it this way and you get it wrong

✕

“They got worse after coming home”

The opposite. Home performance (expected goal difference +0.61) is 1st in the league. What fell was the results.

✕

“The 13-match away run held them back”

23 points from the away run, +1.8 against expectation. The three months on the road went better than expected.

✕

“The defence is weak”

Shots and xG conceded are both the 2nd-fewest in the league, and central entries conceded are the lowest. What is weak is not the suppression but the last cell in front of goal.

✕

“They cannot finish”

Only half true. Away they scored as expected (non-penalty +0.4). The missing goals are concentrated in the central box at home.

✕

“Opponents kept picking the corners”

The placement-expected save rate on opponents’ shots is 70.4%, about league average. Their placement was ordinary.

✕

“They fell apart when Luis Mina left”

There have been only 4 matches since he left. All four home 1-1s came while he was playing (R18–R22).

Summary6 of the last 7 matches at home

The rest of the season, at home

Gimpo play the best home football in K League 2. And at home they have taken 6.8 points fewer than expected. They were not outplayed; the last cell in front of both goals went wrong at the same time.

Of the remaining seven matches (R28–R34), 6 are at the Solter Football Stadium. This season will be decided at home. Three things to watch:

  • The central box at home — xG 9.6, 5 goals there so far. Do they start taking the extra step into the 6-yard box?
  • The last cell on target — home goals conceded from shots on target are +5.1 against placement expectation. Do balls into the same cells start being stopped at the league-average rate?
  • An answer for the settled attack — at home, where weaker sides sit deep, what does a team built to win it and finish it do with the ball? Matches without Luis Mina so far: 4.
