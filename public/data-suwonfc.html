SUWON FC · 2026 · K LEAGUE 2 · 25 MATCHES

They Reach Less, and Shoot the Most

Suwon FC have scored more goals than anyone in K League 2. Yet they reach the opponent’s final third less often than most — 13th of 17. What happens next is different: no team turns those entries into shots more often. I put all 212 league matches on one scale and followed that single line — when it works, and when it stops.

The team that scored the most is not the team that created the most

Suwon FC scored 50 goals in 25 matches, the most of the 17 K League 2 clubs. They are 2nd, 5 points behind the leaders, Suwon Samsung.

Rate every match on the expected goals (xG) of each shot and the order changes. Their non-penalty expected goal difference per match is +0.41 — 4th in the league, behind Suwon Samsung (+0.63), Seoul E-Land (+0.47) and Daegu (+0.43).

Replay every shot 20,000 times at its xG and Suwon FC’s expected points come to 41.7; they have 47. +5.3 points — the third-largest overperformance in the league. Their goals also run +6.7 above the 41.3 xG of their 324 shots, the largest excess in the league.

So is it luck? Re-take the same shots at their xG 100,000 times and 48 or more goals come up 12% of the time. Too large to wave away as luck, too small to call skill. So I changed the question — where is this team different?

MirrorDefence · allowed

They let you in, and do not let you shoot

Apply the same yardstick to the attacks Suwon FC allow and the same shape appears, as if in a mirror.

Final-third entries run 28.9 to 31.9 — opponents get in more often. Yet expected goals run 1.43 to 1.01 in Suwon FC’s favour. Of the shots they allow, 42.3% come from outside the box, 3rd highest in the league, and the xG they allow inside the box, 0.82 per match, is 3rd lowest.

Attack and defence reduce to the same sentence. They give up space, and win beyond the threshold.

MethodTen seconds after the regain

Their fastest attacks lead the league. But they are not where most goals come from

One reason they shoot so often once in is speed. No team generates more xG from shots within ten seconds of winning the ball.

Widen the window to 15 or 20 seconds and they drop to 3rd and 4th. Suwon FC’s weapon is not the counter-attack but the very fast counter-attack. Of their 7.8 ten-second transition xG, 3.69 (47%) comes from Ha Jeong-woo alone, and he also starts more of those chains than anyone (10).

But the story has to pause here. Split their 48 goals (own goals excluded) by where the move began, and attacks that started with a ball win produced 23 goals, 47.9% — below the league average of 53.7%, 11th of 17. The ten-second transition is their sharpest edge, but not their biggest source of goals. Where the rest came from returns later.

Level — the formula at its clearest

Entry → shot 33.1%. Leaving penalty metrics aside, no phase-by-state value in this piece sits further from the league average (z = +2.5). Ten-second transition xG is 1st too. Meanwhile opponents cannot get out: their build-up reaches midfield 55.7% of the time, 2nd lowest in the league.

Leading — the tightest side in the league

In front, they allow 0.65 open-play xG per 90, and an opponent chain that enters their third is worth 0.018 xG. Both are the lowest of the 17. Nor are they sitting back: their own open-play xG in the same minutes is 1.25 per 90, 2nd in the league.

Trailing — every weapon switches off

Behind, final-third entries rank 17th, chains started by a regain 17th, ten-second transition xG 16th. Teams that trail usually push in more often (league average 36.8 entries per 90). Suwon FC rise to 31.1, but that is the fewest of the 17, and shot conversion after entry is 15th. One reading is that an opponent who sits deep leaves fewer balls to win and less space to run into — the event data does not show that directly.

AnswerSet pieces · penalties

And yet they came back — when the ball stopped

Yet in matches where they conceded first, Suwon FC took 1.60 points per game, 2nd in the league. How, with every weapon switched off?

Of the 10 goals they scored while trailing, 3 were penalties and 1 came from set pieces. At the other end, opponents in those minutes created 1.08 open-play xG per 90 and scored only 0.58.

The other end looks the same. Suwon FC allowed 31 set-piece shots worth 2.57 xG and conceded 6 — also the largest excess in the league (3.4% chance by luck alone).

Read these two findings carefully. They surfaced while scanning many metrics, and P-values found that way come out smaller than they should. The samples — 41 set-piece shots, 9 penalties — are small. So I do not write “Suwon FC are strong at set pieces”. I write only that part of this season’s points came from here.
TimeThe first 15 minutes of each half

They score as soon as the door opens

Long spells in front require scoring first. Suwon FC scored first in 58% of the matches that had a goal (5th in the league). I split their goals into 15-minute blocks.

The same shape repeats inside those minutes. Final-third entries per 90 rank 15th in the first half and 16th in the second, yet expected goals rank 2nd in both. In contrast, their xG in minutes 16–30 of the first half, 0.47 per 90, is the lowest in the league. A surge straight after kick-off, then a lull.

DisciplineHypothesis log

What did not survive the test

Hypotheses and readings from this analysis that the data did not support stay in, undeleted. All of them were mine. A rejected hypothesis draws the boundary of the conclusions that survived.

✕

Suwon FC are a counter-attacking team

Revised. Ten-second transition xG is 1st, but at 15 and 20 seconds they are 3rd and 4th. Goals from moves that began with a ball win are 47.9% of their total, below the league average (53.7%). They have the fastest counter-attack, not the most counter-attacking goals.

✕

When behind, they pile on the pressure and turn it round

Rejected. Trailing, they rank 17th for final-third entries and 17th for chains started by a regain. Points won from behind came from dead balls (4 of 10 goals while trailing) and opponents’ poor finishing.

✕

Substitutes change the game

Rejected. Substitutes scored 5 of 49 goals (10.2%), below the league average of 22.9%. Correction — my first count was 11. I missed that on goal rows of the live feed, the “sub in” field holds the assisting player.

✕

They win the ball with a high press

Rejected. Regains in the opponent’s half (x ≥ 60) come 6.75 times per match, 14th in the league; their PPDA of 5.80 (higher = less pressing) is 4th highest. They win the ball in their own half (77% of regains).

✕

Top scorers because of finishing

Unresolved. 48 goals from 324 shots worth 41.3 xG (+6.7) is the league’s biggest excess, but chance alone produces it 12% of the time. The largest single piece of the excess is set pieces (+4.6).

Four traps in the data. ① Goal times in the live feed run about a minute behind the actual shot (median 64 s). Used as they are, the minute before each goal is misfiled as “already ahead”. ② Every match report hides a commented-out sample row (a penalty goal from another match) in its scoring table. Where the team names matched (Suwon FC–Seongnam, 0–0), a phantom goal leaked in. ③ Own goals in the live feed belong to the team that benefited; in the event data they belong to the player’s team. ④ No event data exists for any 5 September match, in K League 1 or 2.
SummaryOn one page

Reach less, shoot the most — and stop when behind

The diagnosis narrows to one line — they reach less, shoot the most often, and close up when ahead. Behind, the formula stops, and the moment the ball stops becomes the lifeline.

What this piece does not say

Whether this shape is the head coach’s design (event data records outcomes, not intent) · whether the set-piece and penalty excess is skill (small samples, found by scanning many metrics) · whether the unbeaten run owes to the fixture list (opponent strength not adjusted) · what happened in R25 away at Yongin (no event data on the portal).
