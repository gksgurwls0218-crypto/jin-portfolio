WHAT THE DATA SAYS · 01
K League 2 2026 · Hwaseong FC · 23 matches · 33,522 events

Hwaseong are not a side that holds the ball. They are a side that spends it.

They attempt the second-fewest passes in the league and get more box shots out of each one than anybody. Their long-pass share is third-highest while their duel count is the lowest and their offside count the highest — the ball goes behind the line, not into a body. And the football only completes itself after the break: 86 shots in first halves, 154 in second, with possession unchanged.

Han HyukjinSeptember 2026Data: K League Data PortalWhat the data says — part 1

The conclusions first

  1. They do not hold the ball. 401.7 passes per match, 16th of 17; 45.7% possession, under 50% in 18 of 23 games. They are 5th in the table.
  2. Each pass is worth more. 2.08 box shots per 100 passes, among the league's best; 70.8% of their shots come from inside the area, the highest in the league.
  3. Long, but into space rather than into a body. 3rd for long-pass share, last for duel volume, first for offsides.
  4. They become a different side after the break. 86 first-half shots become 154; possession goes 45.3% to 46.0%. They shoot more in second halves even when leading at the interval.
  5. Ten scored and one conceded between the 46th and 60th; eight conceded after the 76th. The window they surge in is the window that opens.
  6. Possession does not explain them. Correlation with points −0.05, and the sign flips using first-half possession. They can win without the ball; they do not need to be without it.

1The 5th-placed side that passes least

How much ball you have and how much you make of it are different questions

Hwaseong attempt 401.7 passes per match across 23 games — 16th of 17. They sit 5th in the table, and their completion rate of 83.9% ranks 8th. This is not a side that cannot pass; it is a side that chooses not to.

So divide the passes by what they produce: box shots per 100 passes. Hwaseong return 2.08, the highest in the league — though Daegu at 2.07 and Paju at 2.06 make that effectively a three-way tie. The point is not the rank but the position: near the bottom for volume, at the top for yield.

2Long, but into space rather than into a body

The usual side-effect of playing direct is missing here

Long passes make up 13.4% of Hwaseong's distribution, third-highest in the league. Sides like this normally rack up aerial duels — you hit a target and fight for the second ball. Paju, who top the long-pass share, contest 60.6 duels per match, the most in the league.

Hwaseong contest 40.5, the fewest of all 17. What spikes instead is elsewhere: 2.43 offsides per match, the most in the league, against 1.0 for their opponents in the same fixtures. The average position of those offsides is X 75.2 in attacking direction — right on the opponent's last line.

Long passes many, physical contests fewest in the league, offsides most in the league. Three numbers telling one story — the ball is not played into a body, it is played behind.

3No cheap shots

Ordinary shot volume, extraordinary shot location

Their 11.8 shots per match rank 9th — dead average. But 70.8% of them come from inside the box, the highest in the league and clear of Yongin (69.6%) and Suwon FC (64.8%). Inverted: only 3.3 shots per match from outside the area, 16th of 17. There is no "just hit it" in this side's vocabulary.

4This team becomes a different side after the break

Not more of the ball — different things done with the same amount

Across 23 matches Hwaseong take 86 shots in first halves and 154 in second halves — a 79% increase. The first thing to check is possession, and it barely moves: 45.3% to 46.0%. They are not getting more of the ball.

5The clock of this team

The hour after the break is theirs; the last quarter is the price

Those eight goals after the 76th minute may be a structural cost of this football, or a conditioning and rotation problem. This data cannot separate the two. Doing so would need physical data — distance covered, sprints — which the portal does not carry.

6In from the flanks, finished through the middle

The route in and the place it ends are not the same place

Split the 1,018 passes into the final third by channel: 41% left, 17% centre, 42% right. Their opponents put 23% through the middle in the same phase. Hwaseong do not enter centrally. 83% of entries go down a channel. The goals, though, cluster in the opposite place — eight from the central area in front of goal, four from the penalty-spot area, three from inside the six-yard box.

7Where the 36 goals came from

Not a set-piece side, and heavily reliant on one player

8Possession does not explain this team

Strip out the reverse causation and the conclusion gets careful

In the 35-41% possession band Hwaseong average 2.00 points per match; above 48%, 1.67. Read plainly that says "the less ball, the better." It should not be read that way. Matches like R20 against Daegu — 35% possession, won 5-1 — are cases where the lead produced the low possession, not the other way round.

Take first-half possession, which carries less game state, and the sign flips: 2.00 points per match at 45% or above, 1.36 below it. Across all 23 matches the correlation between possession and points is −0.05, effectively zero. The only quantity that tracks points is xG (+0.54).

10Four matches that show this football best

Chosen by summing eight identity axes as z-scores

The 23 matches were standardised and summed across eight axes: ① low possession ② few passes ③ directness (share of shot-producing possessions taking three passes or fewer) ④ share of entries down the channels ⑤ share of shots in the second half ⑥ offsides ⑦ share of shots inside the box ⑧ mean pass length. The index is my own operational definition, not an official metric.

11So what should be watched

If you are facing them

  1. The space behind the last line. League-most offsides (2.43) is a proxy for how often they run. When that fell to one — R15 against Suwon — their xG was 0.89.
  2. The channels. 83% of their entries into the final third are wide. Winning the flank duel and blocking the cross matters more than sealing the half-space.
  3. The 46th to 60th minute. Ten scored, one conceded in that quarter-hour. Survive the restart and the game looks different.
  4. The opening fifteen. Their weakest window — opponents have already taken 40 shots there against Hwaseong's 26.

If you are Hwaseong

  1. Eight goals conceded after the 76th minute. Cost of the style or a management problem — the two cannot be separated without physical data.
  2. 50% reliance on Plana. Involved in 18 of 36 goals. No alternative shows up in the data if he is missing.
  3. 5.3 corners a match, three goals. The league's third-highest supply, almost unconverted. The cheapest improvement available.
  4. No first-half goal in 12 of 23. Still unresolved whether the second half is well designed or the first half is simply empty.

Appendix — data, verification, limits

Source

Everything comes from the Korea Professional Football League's K League Data Portal (data.kleague.com), public data accessible without a login. No figure here was read off a screenshot; the responses the portal itself uses to draw its pages were parsed directly. Collected 2026-09-04, covering the 23 matches Hwaseong have played through round 24. K League 2 has 17 clubs, so one rests each round — Hwaseong's byes are rounds 16 and 33.

Cross-checks

  • League table vs re-summed match records: exact match (23 games, 11-6-6, 39 points, 36-24)
  • Sum of per-player match data vs team season totals: exact match (271 shots, 9,239 passes, 488 crosses, 202 key passes, 570 tackles, 212 interceptions)
  • Corners and offsides from match records vs season totals: exact match (121 / 56)
  • Opponent goals in the event feed vs official goals against: exact match (24)

Data issues found

  • The 'total' cell of the goals row is wrong in eight of the portal's match records. Summing those cells gives 32-17; re-adding first and second halves gives 36-24, which is what the official table says. Every scoreline here uses the re-added figure. Every other row — shots, corners, fouls, offsides — agreed across all 23 matches.
  • "How many shots Hwaseong took" depends on the source: 271 in the supplementary records, 268 in the event feed, 264 in the portal's xG table, 240 in the match records. The portal runs more than one counting system. League comparisons here use 271, coordinate and time-band analysis uses 268, and the two are never mixed.
  • The event feed tags 34 of the 36 goals (94%). One own goal cannot appear as a Hwaseong event by construction and one is missing. The match record is the authority for goals; the event feed is used only for spatial and time-band work. xG differs too — 33.35 summed from events against the portal's official 30.22. Team-level xG here uses the official value.

My own definitions (not official metrics)

  • Possession phase — one side's spell until the opponent registers two consecutive events or the keeper collects
  • Directness — share of shot-producing phases containing three or fewer passes by that side
  • Wide entry — of passes starting below X 66.7 and ending at or beyond it, the share landing in the outer thirds of Y
  • Identity index — the mean z-score of the eight axes listed in §10

Coordinates

The portal normalises X and Y to 0-100 but keeps them fixed to the pitch, so attacking direction flips at half-time. Every positional figure here was computed after rotating each team so it always attacks towards increasing X. Check: after rotation the mean shot origin sits at X 88.0 for Hwaseong and 86.4 for their opponents — both correct.

What this piece does not answer

  • What actually changes after the break — instruction, opponent fatigue, or substitution. The substitution timing (70th minute on average) does not explain it.
  • Who triggers the runs in behind, and when. Offside counts are only a proxy for frequency.
  • Whether avoiding duels is a design choice or a consequence of the squad's physical profile. Event data cannot tell the two apart.

All three need video and inside information. Marking where the data stops is the rule of this series.
