Problemarriving without shooting

They reach the same place as Gwangju by the opposite route

In the previous episode Gwangju were a team that could not get into the final third. Daejeon are the inverse. Third in the league for getting there, sixth from bottom for shooting once they do.

Converted to shots per final-third action it comes to 0.059, 11th of 12. Gwangju were last for arriving but 5th for shots per arrival. Two clubs build a bottom-half attack for exactly opposite reasons.

The odd part is that when they do shoot, it goes in. The share of their shots taken inside the box is 64.6%, 2nd in the league, and they score on 36.9% of shots on target against a league baseline of 29.6%. Their 38 goals beat their 35.9 xG.

The quality is good and the quantity is not. What Daejeon lack is not good chances but the number of them.

Observationnine silences

The goalless matches all sit on one side

Nine of 27 matches produced nothing. One in three. And all nine of them fall before R19. That is too clean a split to shrug at.

The first suspicion is that opponents sat deep and locked the game. They did not. The average height of opponents' defensive actions was 67.4 in the goalless matches and 68.6 in the rest — effectively identical (measured in Daejeon's attacking direction, higher means a deeper block).

The comparison carries two traps, so it was controlled twice. First, the nine goalless matches cluster in the first half of the season, so they are confounded with time — the comparison was rerun within the first half only. Second, comparing "the 0-0 phase" is circular: in a match they eventually win, that phase ends the moment the goal goes in, so by construction it contains the passage that produced it. Instead the comparison uses a fixed window: the opening 15 minutes, whose length cannot depend on the outcome.

Passes differ by 11% and entries by 11%, while left-side key passes run 0.67 against 6.00. The nine silences had already begun by the fifteenth minute, and they began in one place.

Turnand then it stopped

After R20 there was never another goalless match

1.11 goals per match across the first 19. 2.12 across the last 8. Goalless: 0.

The first question is whether the opponents got easier. The average league position of their opponents was 5.8 before and 6.6 after — near enough the same. More strictly, restricting to the seven clubs they played in both halves (Anyang, Gimcheon, Gangwon, Seoul, Jeju, Ulsan, Gwangju) leaves the result intact.

This is what the rise in xG per shot actually is. Daejeon did not talk themselves out of long shots — from beyond 25m the count went up, from 1.42 to 1.62. What changed is that chances to shoot from inside 12m grew by 81%.

Causewhat changed

There were no signings. The places simply settled

Every player whose minutes jumped in the second half was already at the club in R1. Nobody arrived. What changed is that they started appearing every week.

The key passes moved across the pitch

Splitting where key passes originate into 5 lanes by 3 thirds shows the weight shifting out of the centre and onto both flanks.

And the destination settled too

A cross only becomes a key pass if somebody is there to meet it. In the second half the people standing in the box settled as well — the number of players taking at least 0.25 shots per match from inside the area went from 8 to 10, and box shots themselves from 6.68 to 8.50.

Twistbut it isn't form

Individuals did not sharpen. A combination settled

The natural explanation here is "playing every week brought their sharpness back". That explanation was tested.

Across three seasons of K League 1, 2024–2026, 12 clubs, 24,894 player-matches, the comparison was made within each player: matches he entered having played 180+ minutes across the previous three rounds, against matches he entered on under 120. Differences in ability are controlled away automatically.

By season (2024 −0.29 / 2025 +0.62 / 2026 −0.28) and by position (DF +0.07 / MF −0.21 / FW −0.01) it sits at zero throughout. The faint signal visible in 72 players across six clubs vanished once the sample widened.

So no individual's passing became more accurate. Lee Myung-jae (16) did not start striking the ball better. He was in the same place every week, and the same people were standing in the box, and the crosses began finding somebody.

Form did not attach to the players. It attached to the connection.

The distinction matters in practice. If the players have come good, you wait. If a combination has settled, the job is not to break it.

Limitswhere this could be wrong

Four things

  • The second half is eight matches. Controlling for opponent does not make eight matches into more than eight. Three of them going differently would move these numbers.
  • The direction of cause is not fully closed. Did the places settle and then they played well, or did they play well and get given the places? If the latter, this piece runs backwards. Against that: Lee Myung-jae's (16) poor first half was not confined to his short appearances — in R6, R13, R17 and R19 he played the full match and produced 0–1 key passes.
  • R27 is missing. The raw events for the 5 September match at Bucheon are not yet published. The dataset is R1–R26 plus R28, and the rounds are not contiguous.
  • Event data records only the player who touched the ball. How many players stood in the box, and how they moved, is not counted. "The destination settled" is inferred from the distribution of box shots.

In one sentence: Daejeon's 2026 attack was never about the ability to arrive but about what happened after arriving, and that showed up as nine goalless matches before disappearing after R20. It disappeared not because of new players, but because the ball coming in from the flanks finally began to find somebody.
