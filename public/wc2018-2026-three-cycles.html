Three World Cup cycles · Russia 2018 × Qatar 2022 × North America 2026 · 232 matches

Possession died twice
and came back at the third World Cup

The side with more of the ball won 55% of decided matches in 2018, 47% in 2022 and 76% in 2026. A metric that was a coin toss for two tournaments running became one of the strongest signals at the third. The three tournaments come from different data providers — so I measured 2022 with both rulers to build a bridge, and linked only the metrics that hold up on it.

01 · Method

Three World Cups, two rulers

What the bridge carries, and what it does not

“Who did more of it” (hit rate) survives a change of provider. “How many times” (raw value) does not — the same matches produce 129 pressures on one ruler and 292 on the other. This report links only the former across three tournaments. xG is not linked across tournaments either; the models differ.

France 2018 — a trophy lifted with less of the ball

39.5% against Argentina in the round of 16, 37.1% against Belgium in the semi-final, 34.5% against Croatia in the final. The side with less possession won all three (only against Uruguay in the quarter-final did France have more of the ball, 61.7%). In the 2018 knockouts the side with more of the ball won 4 of 12. FIFA’s Technical Study Group wrote that France “did not rely on ball possession, but rather focused on getting into the opposition’s half as fast as they could.”

What held across all three tournaments: shots on target beat possession (64 → 74 → 86%). The gap between the two lines widened from 9 to 27 points and closed back to 10. 2022 is where possession and threat were furthest apart; 2026 is where they reunited in the same teams.

Spain led possession at two tournaments running and left in the round of 16 both times; at the third they won it from near the top of the possession table.

04 · Conditions for success

How the conditions for success moved

The gap between quarter-finalists and group-stage exits, measured in within-tournament standard scores (z). Because z is scaled by each tournament’s own mean and spread, the size of the gap can sit side by side across rulers (an estimate).

Figure 4 · Gap between last-eight sides and group-stage exits (within-tournament z)

Positive = last-eight sides higher. For xGA, more negative means the last eight conceded fewer chances. 2018·2022 StatsBomb, 2026 FIFA.

2018 — block first

What separated the last eight most was the chances they did not concede (xGA −0.73); the attacking gaps were small. FIFA TSG: “space between the lines was practically non-existent.”

2022 — handle the ball, win on the transition

The pass-completion gap grew (+0.86), but individual matches were decided by counters (hit rate 76%). The last eight waited in a mid-block and struck.

2026 — the gap opened on every axis

Possession, passes, shots on target, xG and xGA all separated by around z 1.0. The widening of the field to 48 teams is mixed in and cannot be separated out.

2018 was the tournament of set pieces, penalties and own goals. The rise in total goals in 2026 came from the group stage alone — from the round of 16 onward, where the best sides meet, scoring matches 2022. And the first goal keeps getting less safe.

Korea’s great World Cup moments came when they gave up the ball — at the two tournaments where possession explained nothing. In 2026, when the tournament rewarded possession, Korea had the ball, but the xG in their two defeats was only 0.87 and 0.74.

Three or four matches per tournament. Read this not as “this is Korean football” but as a moment when the tournament’s direction and Korea’s choices diverged.

08 · Limits

What this comparison cannot answer

Game state

I could not separate whether 2026’s 76% is a team-quality effect or an effect of leading sides no longer sitting deep. StatsBomb 2018·2022 carry an event timeline and can be tested; the 2026 PMSR has none.

No raw values linked

Pressing counts, xG and distance are defined differently by each provider. Only relative measures — hit rates, rank correlations, within-tournament z — link the three tournaments.

48 teams

The widening gaps of 2026 include the effect of the expanded field. The detailed 2022 × 2026 report confirms the direction using only the 26 nations present at both, but the size cannot be separated.

09 · Conclusion

What remains

  1. Possession failed to explain results at two tournaments running and came back in 2026 (55 → 47 → 76%). Both providers give the same value for 2022, so the link holds.
  2. Shots on target beat possession at all three tournaments. “Possession came back” means, precisely, possession carrying threat came back.
  3. The conditions for success moved from blocking (2018) → striking (2022) → holding (2026).
  4. The side with the most possession left early at all three tournaments (Spain R16 · Spain R16 · Türkiye groups). What changed is the champion’s possession — 48.5 → 57.7 → 62.8%.
  5. Penalties fell by more than half in eight years (0.45 → 0.21 per match).
