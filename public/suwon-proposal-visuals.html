Suwon Samsung · Tactical proposal

You do not need more crosses

The club that crosses more than anyone in the league ranks 6th for goals. Setting 21 matches of the 2026 K League 2 season against 96 Big 5 clubs and the 2025 K League technical report narrows the leak to a single phase — the point where a cross becomes a shot. What needs changing is not the volume but the conditions in front of it. Twelve figures for the eight-act proposal, each paired with why this figure — what a first-time viewer reads in three seconds.

ACT 0 · HOOK

Same players, a different cross

Suwon's corners are a weapon the league already acknowledges. The open-play crosses those same players put into that same box are the most numerous in the league — and the least likely to become a shot.

Why this figure Two tactical boards need no explanation. On the left there is no presser and five bodies stand in the box; on the right there are two pressers and two bodies. What the two pictures share is the players; what differs is only the conditions. It closes off the "we lack the personnel" objection in the opening scene.

ACT 1 · PROBLEM

The most produced, the least collected

Why this figure A single shape — a staircase collapsing from top to bottom — finishes the problem statement for the whole proposal. ACT 3 · CAUSE

The cross may be a result, not a choice

Why this figure The staircase only goes one way. Monotonic ordering with no exception is the strongest picture available to a reader who does not read statistics. "The more the ball went through midfield, the more chances followed" ends in four bars.
And this is the one area where Suwon matches the opponent Comparing both teams' pass matrices under the same rule across 21 matches gives Suwon 5.95% against opponents' 5.51% — statistically indistinguishable (t = 0.44). The club that makes more crosses, final-third passes and key passes than anyone in the league is level with its opponents at routing the ball through the centre of midfield. It does track results monotonically, though — wins +2.43pp · draws −0.45pp · defeats −3.70pp. Caution — four defeats is a very small sample. Read the direction, not the size.
What t means t is a ruler for whether a difference is a real one or just the range a figure swings through from match to match. It is the size of the difference measured against that match-to-match spread, and roughly 2 is where one starts calling it a difference. Here t = 0.44 means the gap (+0.44pp) is less than half the match-to-match spread — Suwon and its opponents cannot be told apart on this measure.

ACT 5 · CONTROL

It is the conditions, not the ability

Why this figure The hook thrown in act 0 is paid off here. Two matches a week apart, and two matches whose opponent and output volume coincide — pairs where the players, the coach and the training were the same and the result diverged. A controlled comparison is the cheapest way to close off "we were unlucky".
