A comfortable opening win, and the match where the Guimarães-Cunha role-exchange experiment paid off in front of goal.

<Context heading="Guimarães and Cunha — sharing a pocket, exchanging roles">

Bruno Guimarães wasn't a conventional central midfielder here. He pushed up to forward height into the space Cunha vacated by dropping, and the two met in the same pocket — centre-circle to half-space — collapsing Haiti's marking references. Sometimes Guimarães received and drove while Cunha ran; sometimes the reverse, so Haiti could never predict who would do what. With both sitting between the lines, the gap between Haiti's centre-backs and midfield opened, and Vinícius and Raphinha attacked in behind through it. Read Guimarães as a midfielder and Cunha is free; read him as a forward and Guimarães is free — the inability to close both at once is what produced 1.42 xG (Haiti 0.26).

<Context heading="Lure — pulling Haiti's back five apart">

Brazil ran the same three-step trap on Haiti's 5-4-1 all night. First, Vinícius and Raphinha stretched wide to drag Haiti's wing-backs toward the touchline; when a wing-back followed, one of the five defenders was already out of position. Second, Guimarães read the gap and stepped into the half-space while the ball was on the far side, out of the defense's sightline. Third, the resulting dilemma had no clean answer for a Haitian centre-back: track Guimarães and the middle opens for Cunha; hold the line and Guimarães turns and drives. Either choice favoured Brazil, and the pattern repeating for ninety minutes is what produced 105 completed line breaks — Haiti was made to guess correctly, over and over, with no correct guess available.

</Context>

<Context heading="Buffering — turning pressure into fuel">

Haiti pressed 294 times to Brazil's 195 — 99 more actions trying to break the build-up by volume — and it didn't work, because Cunha absorbed it. Despite starting as the centre-forward, he dropped to central-midfield height to receive as a relay node, already turned before the press arrived, releasing the ball first-time rather than holding it. The pattern didn't slow Brazil down under pressure; it sped them up, and the space that opened as a result flowed the other way. The result: 88% pass completion and 551 total passes against 294 opposition pressures — proof that pressing volume doesn't automatically convert into disruption if the receiving structure is built to absorb it.
