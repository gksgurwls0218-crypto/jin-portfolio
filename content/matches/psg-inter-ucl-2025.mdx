<Context heading="What happens when a system built to defend reference points meets an attack designed to have none?">

Inter arrive as Europe's best-organised block: a back five that defends zones by defending *people entering zones*, screened by a midfield three that closes central lanes by reading the striker's position. Every mechanism in that sentence assumes one thing — that the opponent's attackers have home positions.

PSG's attack has no home positions. Dembélé, Kvaratskhelia and Doué rotate freely through all three front roles; the "4-3-3" on the team sheet resolves, in build-up, into a different geometry almost every sequence. So the question this final poses to the framework: **what happens when a system built to defend reference points meets an attack designed to have none?**

</Context>

<VariationPoint n={3} title="The 7-second reset — counterpress as cycle protection" time="recurring" concepts={["four-phase-cycle"]}>

Every PSG loss of possession triggered an immediate 2–3 man counterpress on the ball. Inter — the competition's most dangerous transition side — barely began a counter-attack all night. Read through the four-phase cycle: PSG's rest-attack runners and counterpress launchpad were positioned *during* possession, so ball loss led either to regain-within-seconds (cycle restarts) or to a tempo-killed Inter build-up against a set block. The phases pay for each other.

</VariationPoint>

<VariationPoint n={4} title="Where the framework strains — Shock without Lure" time="framework check" kind="framework-check" concepts={["lure-shock", "falsification"]}>

My model says Shock is earned by Lure: conditioning first, violation second. PSG complicate this. Their rotation is so continuous that no single expectation ever locks in — they generate buffering without in-match conditioning, because *the opponent is never given time to form a prior at all*. Two honest readings: (a) the Lure operated at season scale — eight months of film is the conditioning; (b) permanent mutation is a distinct, Lure-less route to buffering available only to squads with elite one-v-one quality everywhere. I hold both, and note the cost: a system that skips structured conditioning also skips structured protection — the 5-4 semi-final against Bayern showed the same design conceding four in transition.

</VariationPoint>

</VariationPoints>
