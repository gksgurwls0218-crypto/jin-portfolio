<Context heading="A formation is not an attacking structure">

The team sheet said 4-3-3 and 4-2-3-1; neither described what happened. The gap between each player's starting position (the dashed nodes) and where he actually received the ball (the solid nodes, weighted by pass frequency) is where the real roles live. In modern football a formation label is a starting point, not a conclusion — the drift between the two is the analysis.

</Context>

<Context heading="The build-up core is the first structure of counter-blocking">

PSG took a Mendes–Pacho–Marquinhos back three with João Neves as the anchor — a 3-1 build-up core. Bayern formed theirs with an Upamecano–Tah–Davies back three and a Kimmich–Pavlović double pivot. In both cases build-up and defence are designed from the same positions: the shape that starts the attack is also the shape that screens the counter. That is why the core is the first line of rest-defence, not just a passing device.

</Context>

<Context heading="Rest-attack — positioning to counter while still defending">

Both sides carried a rest-attack structure built from three roles. The **Wide Runner** stretches wide and forces the opponent's line to spread. The **Binder** is an anchor-type forward who pins defenders inside a zone. The **Connector** links the ball-winning area to the forwards. PSG kept the Wide Runner and Binder in fixed positions so the Connector could launch the transition instantly. Bayern let Kane and Kimmich act as Connectors, with distribution changing according to where the advanced attackers stood — Kane dropping deep as the Binder to open space for Olise, Musiala and Díaz to run into.

</Context>

<Context heading="What actually happened">

In a record-breaking nine-goal first leg, PSG were 5-2 up early in the second half — Kvaratskhelia (24', 56') and Dembélé (45+5' pen, 58') scoring twice each off the wing-cut-in and one-two combinations that won last season's final, with João Neves (33') nodding in the third — after Kane's 17' penalty and Olise's 41' had twice pulled Bayern level. Then Bayern's Upamecano (65') and a superb Luis Díaz strike (68') hauled it back to 5-4. Bayern generated the higher xG (2.5 to 1.9) from 22 shots at roughly 40% conversion, but PSG's finishing ran clear of their xG: individual quality, an in-position mutation, breaking the defensive structure.

</Context>

<Context heading="In the framework — and the caveat">

Bayern's four transition goals are the Wide Runner / Binder / Connector structure working at the very top level — rest-attack, proven. PSG, meanwhile, skipped Phase 1 (the repeated conditioning) and went straight to the Shock, exploiting the fact that a different-league Bayern had not been conditioned to their pattern; the season-scale Lure was already banked. But nine goals is variation overload. Neither side ran a real defensive phase, and PSG's shape without the ball is unstable — when the press is beaten there is no structure behind it. An attacking philosophy needs a symmetric defensive base, and here that balance was broken. (Caveat: Kompany was absent, so this is a limited sample of Bayern's system.)

</Context>
