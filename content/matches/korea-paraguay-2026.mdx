<Context heading="A hybrid that lived on roles, not on a formation label">

Korea lined up in a 3-4-3 against Paraguay's 4-2-3-1, but the name on the sheet mattered less than the fluidity of the roles — the build-up and defensive shapes reshaped between phases, and that fluidity is where the goals came from.

</Context>

<Context heading="Hwang In-beom — the holding midfielder as chief variable">

Hwang cycled through three roles inside one match. As the pivot he sat in front of the back line and distributed; he dropped into the back line to form a build shape and add an option; and at the right moments he broke the line to thicken the midfield and join the attack. A holding midfielder rotating through several roles is the on-pitch reading of the principle that the player with the most touches should carry the most variation.

</Context>

<Context heading="Lee Kang-in — self-limitation, then the decisive pass">

Every time Lee Kang-in dropped to help the build, a chain reaction freed the right: Paraguay tracked him, their marking shifted, and space opened out wide. Lee Dong-gyeong and Kim Moon-hwan also swapped roles to scramble the marking references. The pay-off came late — Lee Kang-in's creativity produced the assist for substitute Oh Hyun-gyu's 75' goal, the moment that killed the game off at 2-0.

</Context>

<Context heading="Overload the left, strike — and finish">

The structure was the familiar one: overload one side to lean Paraguay's block across, then attack the space that opened. This time it converted early — Eom Ji-sung's 15' opener came out of Korea's wide, penetrative play down the flanks, exactly the isolation the overload is designed to create. Paraguay actually had more shots (10 to 8), but Korea's were the higher-quality ones (3 on target to 1), and the two that mattered came from the designed pattern.

</Context>

<Context heading="In the framework">

The multi-positionality was real — Hwang In-beom's three roles, Lee Kang-in's self-limitation, the Lee Dong-gyeong / Kim Moon-hwan switch — and here it did what it is supposed to do: give Paraguay's 4-2-3-1 no stable reference point to mark, then convert. One holding-midfielder rotation and one moment of individual creativity from Lee Kang-in were enough to turn role fluidity into two goals.

</Context>
