<Context heading="A hybrid that lived on roles, not on a formation label">

Korea built from a 3-1 that flipped to a 3-2, with the roles reshaping between the in- and out-of-possession phases. The name on the team sheet mattered less than the fluidity of the roles — which is exactly where the interesting football, and the eventual limit, both showed up.

</Context>

<Context heading="Hwang In-beom — the holding midfielder as chief variable">

Hwang cycled through three roles inside one match. As the pivot of a 3-2 core he sat in front of the two stoppers and distributed. He then dropped into the back line to form a 3-3 / 3-2 build shape, adding an option in possession. And at the right moments he broke the line and pushed on, thickening the midfield and joining the attack. A holding midfielder rotating through several roles is the on-pitch reading of the principle that the player with the most touches should carry the most variation.

</Context>

<Context heading="Lee Kang-in — self-limitation that opened the right">

Every time Lee dropped deep to join the build-up, a chain reaction freed Kim Moon-hwan higher up: Paraguay tracked Lee, their marking shifted, and an isolation lane opened for Kim on the right — the Plan A. One player changing position redistributing the whole team's balance is multi-positionality made concrete. Lee Dong-gyeong and Kim Moon-hwan also swapped roles at times, scrambling Paraguay's marking references — the wide expression of the hybrid shape.

</Context>

<Context heading="The team-variable: overload the left, strike the right">

The structure repeated: overload the left again and again (Plan A−) until Paraguay's block leaned that way (conditioning), then switch the point of attack to Kim Moon-hwan's isolated one-v-one on the right (Plan A). It maps directly onto the Plan A− → Plan A mechanism. The problem arrived at the next step.

</Context>

<Context heading="The limit — no depth of options">

When the isolation was shut down, there was no alternative. The pattern became "a → blocked → a again": even after Paraguay had clearly learned to load the right, the same single exit was repeated, the whole attack converging on one outlet. This is a shortage of depth of options. The multi-positionality was real — Hwang's three roles, Lee's self-limitation, the Lee Dong-gyeong / Kim Moon-hwan switch — but every role combination funnelled into one attacking exit. When role changes don't produce genuinely different choices, multi-positionality becomes a single plan wearing several disguises.

</Context>
